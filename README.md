## What this is

An offline guide and tracker for all 55 PlayStation 5 trophies of Grand Theft Auto VI (1 platinum, 2 gold, 6 silver, 46 bronze). Every trophy shows its official name, description and grade, plus a guide written for this page in our own words: what to do, what to bring, steps to follow, a practical tip, and a Sources line naming where each fact comes from.

The game released on 19 November 2026 and the author has not played it. The guides are therefore built only from the official descriptions and the PSN progress targets: where a specific place or step is not pinned down by a source, the guide says so plainly instead of inventing a walkthrough. A cited guide beats an uncited one, however detailed — so each trophy carries its sources beside it.

What the app does:

- **Checklist** — tick trophies off; progress is saved in the browser (localStorage). Export it as JSON or as a plain-text checklist, import it back from JSON, or copy a one-line progress summary to share.
- **Next up** — the easiest trophy you have not earned yet, picked by difficulty then hours, with a button that opens its guide. Press `n` anywhere to jump to it.
- **Surprise me** — a random unearned trophy, for when the list feels too long to choose from.
- **Filters and sorting** — by kind (story, mission, activity, collectible, combat & crime, exploration, partner, money & property), by grade, by earned/unearned, by missable only, and by text search across names, descriptions, guides and steps. Sort by list order, easiest first, quickest first, or name. Press `/` to jump to the search box.
- **Difficulty and time** — every trophy carries a difficulty rating (1–5 stars, the author's own judgement) and an estimated time in hours, so the next trophy to chase is easy to pick.
- **By phase** — the same 55 trophies grouped by the roadmap's seven phases, so the order to earn them in is visible at a glance. Each phase has a button that ticks all its trophies at once.
- **Stats** — a dashboard with counts by grade and by kind, the hours left on the unearned trophies, and a platinum readiness figure.
- **Spoiler setting** — hides the names, descriptions and guides of story trophies until they are earned. On by default.
- **Roadmap to the platinum** — seven phases in order: story, stranger strands, activities, collectibles and exploration, crime and partner, money and property, then the 100% cleanup and the platinum. Each phase shows its trophies, its hours and its own progress. Reach it from the button above the list, or directly at `#roadmap`.
- **Missables** — the trophies whose descriptions set a condition that can fail on an attempt (an undetected robbery, a back-to-back chain, a single use of Combat Focus), each with a note on how to set the attempt up; everything else is listed as safe to leave for later, with the warning that mission replay has not yet been confirmed in-game. Reach it from the button above the list, or directly at `#missables`.
- **Progress header** — X/55 earned, counts by grade, and a platinum progress bar.
- **Detail pane** — click any trophy for its full guide, what to bring, steps, tip, PSN target, missable note and sources.

The look is our own: a light paper design with a coral accent and serif display headings. No Rockstar Games logo or official artwork anywhere.

| 5 — Crime and partner | Robberies, escapes, partner jobs | 12 | ~24 |

Hours are the author's estimates and overlap heavily — one drive can feed several counts at once, so the true total is far less than the sum. The two golds (the final mission and 100% completion) are the real work; clear the phases in order and the platinum follows.

## Tips for the platinum hunt

- **Save before every timing-sensitive attempt.** The seven missable-flagged trophies fail if the attempt goes wrong — an undetected robbery, a back-to-back chain, a single Combat Focus. A save before the attempt is the whole strategy.
- **Do the counted grinds together.** Stunts, races, meditation, WAiNK spots, vehicles and cards all live on the same map — one tour feeds several counters.
- **Launder and spend as you go.** The $250,000 laundering count and the $100,000 spending count are long; letting them fill up while you play beats saving them for the end.
- **Let the choice counter fill itself.** 500 choices sounds like a lot, but it climbs on its own while you play the story — do not chase it.

## How the difficulty ratings work

Every trophy carries a difficulty rating from 1 to 5 stars and an estimated time in hours. Both are the author's own judgement, not official figures:

- **1 star (Easy)** — happens on its own while playing, or takes one short attempt.
- **2 stars (Fair)** — a little effort: one activity, one short chain, or a modest count.
- **3 stars (Tricky)** — a real grind or a condition that needs care, like an undetected robbery.
- **4 stars (Hard)** — a long count (hundreds of repetitions) or a chain that takes hours.
- **5 stars (Brutal)** — the longest grinds: the 100% completion count and the final mission.

The Next up box uses these ratings to suggest the easiest trophy left, breaking ties by hours. Sort by "Easiest first" or "Quickest first" to plan your own order.

## Known limitations

- **The game is new and the author has not played it.** GTA VI released on 19 November 2026. The guides are built only from the official trophy descriptions and the PSN progress targets; they are a reading of the list, not a walkthrough from play.
- **Some places and steps are not pinned down.** Where a source does not name the place or the step, the guide says so in the text and the risk note flags the uncertainty — it does not invent a location or a method.
- **Mission replay is unconfirmed.** Nothing in the official list says whether a mission can be replayed, so the missable flags are a warning based on the description's condition, not a verdict from play.
- **Difficulty and hours are estimates.** They are the author's own judgement, meant to help pick the next trophy, not a promise of how long a grind will take.
- **The guide is in English only.** The official trophy names and descriptions are quoted in English; the guides and tips are written in English as well.

## Repository layout

- `app/` — the whole guide: `index.html`, `app.js`, `style.css` and `data/trophies.json`. Everything the page loads lives here, so it runs offline.
- `screenshots/` — the four screenshots the entry hands in (checklist, detail pane, roadmap, stats).
- `main.ts` — the build entry point: checks the data and the size caps, stages `app/` and `screenshots/`, writes `dossier.json`.
- `serve.ts` — the local preview server (`node serve.ts`, then http://localhost:8080).
- `vendor/makefun-frontend.ts` — the kit's vendored library, unchanged.
- `shots.json` — the shot list for the four screenshots, with a note on what each one shows.

## How to run offline

No build step, no network. Serve `app/` with any static server:

```sh
node serve.ts
```

Then open <http://localhost:8080> in desktop Chrome. Turn the network off and it still works — every file the app loads lives under `app/`, and the only request it makes at run time is a relative fetch of `app/data/trophies.json`.

## How to hand it in

```sh
mkdir -p out && MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
```

That checks the trophy data (55 trophies, each with a name and a guide, every phase in 1–7, every difficulty in 1–5), checks the scorer's size caps, verifies each screenshot is a real JPEG or PNG, copies `app/` and `screenshots/` to the output directory and writes `dossier.json` with the vendored make.fun library (`vendor/makefun-frontend.ts`, unchanged from the kit).

The build is deterministic: run it twice into two directories and the two `dossier.json` files are byte-identical. If they differ, something in the data changed between runs.
